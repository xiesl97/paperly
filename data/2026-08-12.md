Abstract: I introduce the last crossing of the photon-counting barrier as a statistic for analytical models of cosmic reionization. Because ionizing galaxies are biased tracers of density and their photons propagate through gas of spatially varying opacity, the galaxy-IGM connection requires statistics beyond the global ionized fraction. The excursion-set model of Furlanetto et al. (2004) identifies the ionized-bubble scale around a point with the first crossing of a photon-counting barrier. Their combination of low gas density and a deficit of nearby sources suggests that Ly$α$ transmission spikes may preferentially arise in these regions: low density reduces the opacity, while sources on larger scales maintain the ionized state. The last-crossing picture connects the internal source geometry of ionized regions to a testable prediction for radial galaxy distributions around transmission-selected IGM locations.
